The story of recovery

Recovery has become a buzzword in fitness — but what does it actually mean? Understanding the relationship between stress, energy, and adaptation is the foundation of everything Morpheus does.

Recovery has become a buzzword in fitness over the last few years, but what does it really mean? Does a higher recovery score mean you can train hard — and a lower one mean you should stay home? To get to the bottom of how Morpheus works, we have to go back to two fundamentals: stress and energy.

How the body adapts to stress (and why it's so important)

At the very heart of fitness — and survival itself — is the concept of adaptability. In simplest terms, adaptation is the process the body goes through to become more fit to handle the demands of its environment.

This is how training works. By lifting weights, doing cardiovascular conditioning, practicing a skill, or playing a sport, you're creating a specific environment that your body has to adapt to. You lift heavy weights, it gets stronger. You run long distances, it gets more efficient. You practice a movement, your technique improves.

This is nothing more than the body's adaptive mechanisms at work. There are two parts to this process: stress and recovery. When you're training, your stress-response system is working hard to crank up energy production. The more force and power your muscles produce, the more energy they need.

Once the workout is over, that's when recovery begins. The most important thing to understand about recovery is that — just like stress — it's all about energy. The body needs energy to repair and rebuild stressed muscle tissue, to add new mitochondria, and to create and reinforce the neural pathways that improve technique and skill. This use of energy is what we call recovery.

Recovery is the process of using energy to adapt to the stress of our environment. It's this process that turns the workouts we do into improvements in strength, power, hypertrophy, body comp, skill, and performance.

— Joel Jamieson, Founder of Morpheus

The problem with recovery

Your body constantly cycles through periods of stress followed by time to recover. In a perfect world, you'd have all the energy you need to fully adapt to each period. You'd make consistent improvements in fitness. You'd never feel tired, run down, or lack the motivation to train. You'd never get sick.

The problem is we don't live in that perfect world. In the real world, our bodies can only produce a fixed amount of energy each day — no matter how much we eat or sleep. The mental stress of life adds up quickly. It can be all too easy to put your body under more stress than it has the energy to adapt to. When this happens, you put yourself into a recovery debt.

If a recovery debt is small, it most often leads to frustrating plateaus — you're putting in the work but not seeing improvement. Over time, if the balance isn't restored, the body will fight back. You'll feel more fatigued, be more likely to get injured, and crave foods you know you shouldn't eat.

Fortunately, there's a clear way to prevent all of this.

The Morpheus recovery score

A lot of people have been led to believe that a low recovery score means their body can't train hard or perform well. They're often confused when they get a low score even though they feel fine — or surprised when they choose to train hard anyway and hit a PR.

A recovery score is not a predictor of what your body is capable of at any given moment. The most accurate way to read it — particularly the score Morpheus gives you — is as a gauge of the balance between the energy you've been spending on stress versus recovery over the last few days.

If your score is low on a given day, it doesn't mean you can't train hard. It means you have less energy available to adapt to that hard session. Train hard on a low score, and it'll take you longer to recover from it than if you'd started from a higher baseline.

Using Morpheus to balance stress and recovery

Getting into a recovery debt doesn't happen in a single day or even a week — it creeps up without you realizing it. When you keep adding stress on top of stress without allowing enough time and energy for recovery, that's when the real damage happens.

When your recovery score is consistently low, it's a sign your body has been spending more time dealing with stress than recovering from it. Because gains in fitness happen during recovery, a chronically low score also means you're leaving results on the table.

Using the Morpheus recovery score to guide your training — and your daily lifestyle — is the key to achieving long-term gains in health, fitness, and performance. The rest of this section will teach you how to measure your recovery each day, and how to use Morpheus to improve it.
